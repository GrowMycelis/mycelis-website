Controls and harvest estimates

Mycelis Base is designed with one start/stop button, a short countdown before activation and a flashing red error indicator. Hold the button for three seconds to toggle the light. Pro uses a touchscreen for start/stop, crop selection, environmental readings and harvest estimates.

Mycelis Pro estimates timing using species and room temperature. Base does not provide harvest predictions. Accuracy is not yet validated. These are guides, not guaranteed harvest dates.

What does Mycelis sense and control?

Automatic water delivery, humidity control and fan control are intended for both models. Mycelis measures temperature and humidity: Base uses readings internally where needed; Pro displays them. Measuring temperature does not mean regulating it. Neither model has active heating or cooling.

Offline use and power interruptions

No app or cloud connection is required for growing. Pro can optionally use Wi-Fi for date/time, rather than remote control. No remote monitoring or AI growing features are included at launch.

Power recovery is a design intention, not a tested reliability feature. Base is intended to return in a paused state after power is restored. Pro is intended to recover stored information and obtain date/time through optional Wi-Fi or request manual input. Automatic grow resumption is not promised; this behaviour still needs testing.

Why oyster mushrooms?

Something worth cooking.

A hot pan, a little oil, garlic if you like. Or add them to noodles, eggs or a risotto. Growing them is interesting; having another ingredient to cook with is the reason to do it.

Fibre.

Reference data for raw oyster mushrooms include dietary fibre. How much ends up in a meal depends on the portion and how you prepare it. [1]

B vitamins.

The same data list riboflavin (B2) and niacin (B3). They're useful details about the food, not a nutrient specification for a Mycelis cartridge. [1]

Pink, specifically.

Pink oyster is the variety in our prototype. Published research on pink oysters has measured thiamine (B1) and riboflavin (B2), with levels affected by the growing substrate. That study didn't test a Mycelis harvest. [2]

We haven't lab-tested the nutrients in a Mycelis harvest. General oyster-mushroom data aren't a pink-oyster nutrition label. Species, growing conditions, portion and cooking all matter; we don't claim that pink is healthier than blue or grey, or that the machine makes mushrooms more nutritious.

The intended Mycelis routine

How a grow works

This is the intended design sequence, not final validated customer instructions. Our planned refills hold a prepared, colonised block in a plastic bag inside a cardboard shell. Compatible homemade cartridges are intended to be supported too.

  1. 01

    Prepare and load.

    Shortly before use, the intended setup is to tear off one cardboard cover with your thumb and cut an X-shaped opening in the plastic bag beneath it. Then insert the cartridge and fill the reservoir.

  2. 02

    Watch it.

    Start with the Base button or Pro touchscreen. Both are intended to automate water delivery, humidity and fan control. Base lighting is manually toggled; Pro adds scheduling and harvest estimates. You'll still check the grow. Automated growth has been demonstrated, with airflow still being refined.

  3. 03

    Harvest, then turn.

    After the first harvest, remove the opposite cardboard cover and cut a fresh X in the bag. Rotate the cartridge 180° to try for further growth. The intended timing is soon after harvest, ideally the same day; a second harvest isn't guaranteed.

Loading a cartridge

Three removable, magnetic windows.

All three window panels are removable and magnetically attached in the intended Mycelis design. A side window comes away to let you slide the cartridge into place; the front window is removable too.

That means leaving space beside the machine for loading and removal. Final clearances, cartridge preparation and cleaning instructions still need validation.

The loading image is a generated concept, not a record of a completed test. The grow in the separate prototype photo is pink oyster.

About using Mycelis

A few practical questions

Can I use my own mushroom cartridges in Mycelis?

Compatible homemade cartridges are intended to be supported. We'll publish the validated fit, ventilation, drainage and material requirements before launch. That doesn't mean every grow bag or third-party cartridge will be suitable.

Do I have to buy your refills or take out a subscription?

No subscription will be required. Our ready-to-grow cartridges are being developed for convenience, alongside support for compatible DIY cartridges. The cartridge range and any future refill services are still being worked out.

Which oyster varieties can I choose?

Blue Oyster, Italian Grey Oyster and Pink Oyster have all been grown successfully during development and are our intended launch trio. They share the same pricing, with mix-and-match packs planned. That doesn't guarantee a particular harvest or confirm repeatable second-face results for each variety.

What happens to the cartridge after use?

The entire official cartridge is single-use and replaced when you're finished with it. There is no reusable holder to transfer. The intended second-face grow uses that same cartridge before disposal; single-use doesn't mean throwing it away after the first harvest.

The intended cardboard shell and plastic substrate bag are designed to separate after use. Cardboard recycling, organic-bin acceptance and spent-substrate use in soil still need verification. We aren't claiming the whole cartridge is recyclable or compostable. We'll publish confirmed end-of-use and cleaning guidance before launch.

What will cartridge labels include?

Labels are planned to identify the variety and packaging date, with storage instructions, expiry guidance and growing instructions. Storage temperatures, dates and any necessary safety warnings still need testing and appropriate review before printing.

Batch numbers for traceability, a QR link to illustrated instructions, substrate ingredients and packaging-separation/disposal guidance are proposed additions. The labels and guide link aren't finalised yet.

Will every model have a screen or need an app?

Base has one start/stop button and no screen; Pro uses a touchscreen. Neither needs an app or a cloud connection for growing. Pro's optional Wi-Fi is for date/time, not remote monitoring. See the model comparison for intended controls, estimates and untested power-recovery behaviour.

How much room will it need?

A kitchen bench is the intention, with space at the side to load the cartridge. Final dimensions aren't published yet, so please wait for those before planning a tight spot for it.

Are these more nutritious than mushrooms from a growing kit?

We haven't tested that. Mycelis is being designed to bring the chamber and growing controls into one appliance. That isn't evidence of better nutrition than a normal kit, or of a particular health outcome.

Can I eat oyster mushrooms raw?

Cook oyster mushrooms before eating them, and don't eat mushrooms that are rotting or spoiled. Growing indoors doesn't replace careful food handling. We'll publish the finished harvest and cleaning instructions before launch. University of Florida oyster-mushroom guidance.

When can I buy the machine or cartridges?

They aren't on sale yet. The cartridge prices above are indicative while we refine early in-house production. Final availability, collection or delivery options and release timing still need confirmation. Join the waitlist to hear when there's more to share.
