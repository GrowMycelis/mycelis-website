Mycelis

Grow oyster mushrooms on your kitchen bench.

Mycelis is a countertop chamber for growing oyster mushrooms in a cartridge. We're developing convenient ready-to-grow refills, while designing for compatible homemade cartridges too.

The prototype is growing pink oysters now. We're nearly ready to show it in person.

Why oyster mushrooms?

Something worth cooking.

A hot pan, a little oil, garlic if you like. Or add them to noodles, eggs or a risotto. Growing them is interesting; having another ingredient to cook with is the reason to do it.

Fibre.

Reference data for raw oyster mushrooms include dietary fibre. How much ends up in a meal depends on the portion and how you prepare it. [1]

B vitamins.

The same data list riboflavin (B2) and niacin (B3). They're useful details about the food, not a nutrient specification for a Mycelis cartridge. [1]

Pink, specifically.

Pink oyster is the variety in our prototype. Published research on pink oysters has measured thiamine (B1) and riboflavin (B2), with levels affected by the growing substrate. It hasn't tested our grow. [2]

We haven't lab-tested the nutrients in a Mycelis harvest. General oyster-mushroom data aren't a pink-oyster nutrition label. Species, growing conditions, portion and cooking all matter; we don't claim that pink is healthier than blue or grey, or that the machine makes mushrooms more nutritious.

Sources & what they tell us
  1. Fulgoni & Agarwal, 2021 — oyster-mushroom reference nutrients. Table 1 reproduces USDA composition data for raw oyster mushrooms. This Mushroom Council-funded dietary-modelling study wasn't a test of Mycelis or its cartridges.
  2. Zięba and colleagues, 2021 — pink oyster cultivation. A controlled study of Pleurotus djamor, including magnesium-supplemented substrates. Its vitamin measurements don't establish the composition of our harvests or a health benefit from eating them.

How Mycelis works

How a grow works

Our ready-to-grow cartridges are planned to hold a prepared grow block, so you can start without mixing substrate or inoculating a block at home. Compatible homemade cartridges are intended to be supported as another option.

  1. 01

    Load it.

    Pull off the magnetic side window, slide the cartridge into the chamber, and put the window back. Fill the reservoir before starting the grow.

  2. 02

    Watch it.

    The chamber is being designed to support light, airflow and humidity. The prototype's screen shows readings and grow progress; final controls and features will depend on the model.

  3. 03

    Pick it.

    Harvest the mushrooms when they're ready. After the grow, remove the used cartridge and clean the chamber before starting another.

Loading a cartridge

The window comes off from the side.

The side window is held on with magnets. It lifts away completely, giving you room to slide the cardboard cartridge into place. The front window stays where it is.

That also means leaving some space beside the machine. You should be able to load and remove a cartridge without having to rearrange the kitchen every time.

We're still working through the final cartridge range and care instructions. The grow in the prototype photo is pink oyster.

Ready-to-grow or homemade

Grow your way.

Prefer a prepared cartridge? Our refills are being developed for that convenience. Enjoy making your own? We're designing Mycelis to support compatible homemade mushroom cartridges too.

The aim is a choice, not dependence on our consumables. Official refills are intended to offer a convenient, tested setup; compatible DIY cartridges give you room to experiment. No subscription will be required.

About using Mycelis

A few practical questions

Can I use my own mushroom cartridges in Mycelis?

Compatible homemade cartridges are intended to be supported. We'll publish the validated fit, ventilation, drainage and material requirements before launch. That doesn't mean every grow bag or third-party cartridge will be suitable.

Do I have to buy your refills or take out a subscription?

No subscription will be required. Our ready-to-grow cartridges are being developed for convenience, alongside support for compatible DIY cartridges. The cartridge range and any future refill services are still being worked out.

Will every model have a screen or need an app?

The current prototype has a screen for temperature, humidity and grow progress. It isn't a specification for every production model. Final Base and Pro controls, screen features and any app requirements will be confirmed before launch.

How much room will it need?

A kitchen bench is the intention, with space at the side to load the cartridge. Final dimensions aren't published yet, so please wait for those before planning a tight spot for it.

Are these more nutritious than mushrooms from a growing kit?

We haven't tested that. Mycelis is being designed to bring the chamber, readings and controls into one appliance. That isn't evidence of better nutrition than a normal kit, or of a particular health outcome.

Can I eat oyster mushrooms raw?

Cook oyster mushrooms before eating them, and don't eat mushrooms that are rotting or spoiled. Growing indoors doesn't replace careful food handling. We'll publish the finished harvest and cleaning instructions before launch. University of Florida oyster-mushroom guidance.

When can I buy the machine or cartridges?

They aren't on sale yet. Machine pricing, cartridge options and release timing still need to be confirmed. We'll share those details through the mailing list and this site.
