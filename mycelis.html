How it's designed to work

From cartridge to skillet.

The chamber brings watering, humidity and airflow together so there's less to manage by hand.

  1. 01

    Load a cartridge.

    Fill the reservoir, prepare one face of the cartridge, then slide it in through a removable side window.

  2. 02

    Start the grow.

    Use the Classic button or Pro touchscreen. The machine is designed to manage water, humidity and airflow while you keep an eye on the crop.

  3. 03

    Harvest, then turn.

    Grow one face at a time. After harvesting, prepare the other face and turn the cartridge to try for another harvest. A second crop isn't guaranteed.

Side-loading cartridge

Easy access to the grow.

The design has three removable magnetic windows. Lift off a side window to slide the cartridge in, or remove the front window to reach into the chamber.

Leave room beside the appliance for loading. Final clearances will be confirmed after testing.

Controls and harvest estimates

Classic uses a tactile start/stop button; hold it for three seconds to toggle the light. Pro uses a touchscreen for crop selection, temperature and humidity readings, light scheduling and harvest estimates.

Pro estimates use species and room temperature as a guide to timing, rather than a guaranteed harvest date.

What does Mycelis sense and control?

Automatic water delivery, humidity control and fan control are intended for both models. Mycelis measures temperature and humidity: Classic uses readings internally where needed; Pro displays them. Measuring temperature does not mean regulating it. Neither model has active heating or cooling.

Why oyster mushrooms?

Something worth cooking.

A hot pan, a little oil, garlic if you like. Or add them to noodles, eggs or a risotto. Growing them is interesting; having another ingredient to cook with is the reason to do it.

Fibre.

Reference data for raw oyster mushrooms include dietary fibre. How much ends up in a meal depends on the portion and how you prepare it. [1]

B vitamins.

The same data list riboflavin (B2) and niacin (B3). They're useful details about the food, not a nutrient specification for a Mycelis cartridge. [1]

Pink, specifically.

Pink oyster is the variety in our prototype. Published research on pink oysters has measured thiamine (B1) and riboflavin (B2), with levels affected by the growing substrate. That study didn't test a Mycelis harvest. [2]

Published food research, not measurements from a Mycelis harvest. Nutrition varies by crop, growing conditions and what you serve.

A few practical questions

Do I have to use both sides of the cartridge?

No. Start with one fruiting face. Turning the cartridge after harvesting is optional, and the second face may not fruit.

How should I prepare oyster mushrooms to eat?

Cook oyster mushrooms before eating them, using clean hands and utensils. Use fresh mushrooms and discard any that are spoiled. See the University of Florida oyster-mushroom guidance for more information.

Can I grow without Wi-Fi?

Yes. No app or cloud connection is required for growing. Wi-Fi on Pro is optional for setting the date and time.
