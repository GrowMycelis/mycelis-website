About Mycelis and Virelis

We're building these in Christchurch.

Mycelis is for oyster mushrooms. Virelis is for microgreens. The aim is to make growing a little food at home easier to fit into an ordinary kitchen.

As of 17 November 2026, both Pro models are nearing prototype completion and both Base models are in development. Neither Pro is ready for production. This site shares the work so far and the designs we are working towards.

What we're trying to get right

It has to work on a kitchen bench.

There's more to that than making a nice box. Can you get a cartridge in and out easily? Can you reach the lower tray? Are the controls easy to understand?

Mycelis loads from the side through a removable magnetic window. Virelis needs room in front so you can take the trays out. Where the machine sits matters as much as how it looks.

Those are the everyday details we're paying attention to alongside the lights and growing environment. We're also designing for compatible DIY consumables, so convenience doesn't have to mean depending on our refills.

Living with a grower

More than the first harvest.

Still a little hands-on.

Automatic growing doesn't mean leaving it alone. You'll still check the grow, top up water as directed, harvest, and clean the chamber or trays between cycles. We're working through those routines alongside the hardware; final care instructions will follow testing.

A choice of refills.

We're developing colonised mushroom cartridges for Mycelis and pre-seeded mats for Virelis. Compatible homemade refills are part of the design too. You won't need a subscription to use either machine; we'll publish dimensions and compatibility guidelines once they're validated.

Before either goes on sale

Some details are still open.

Both machines are planned in Base and Pro versions. Base is intended to retain automatic growing functions, with tactile buttons and no screen. Pro is planned with a screen, growth-duration estimates and automatic light cycles; those extra features are excluded from Base.

The remaining specifications, prediction accuracy, appliance dimensions and release timing still need validation. Cleaning guidance and electricity-use figures need testing too. We've also shared preliminary Virelis refill and optional subscription plans, not products you can buy yet. The prototype photos show screen-equipped builds, not screenless Base models.

Sunday market demonstrations are planned, but no event or date is confirmed. Visitors can currently register interest, not book a demonstration or reserve a machine. If you have a question, we'd like to hear from you.
