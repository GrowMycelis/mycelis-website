Virelis

A countertop microgreens grower

A few trays of greens, close to the kitchen.

A compact, three-tier countertop appliance designed to automate watering and help grow fresh microgreens indoors.

The aim is fewer things to keep track of, in an appliance that feels at home in the kitchen. Pre-seeded refill mats and support for compatible homemade mats are in development too.

As of 17 November 2026, the Pro prototype is nearing completion and Base is in development. Only Mixed Greens has been grown in Virelis so far. Automatic watering is incomplete; we're improving spray coverage and drip control. Nothing is on sale yet.

Controls and harvest estimates

Virelis Base has three independent light controls, one per tier, with no screen. Manual light control remains the current Base specification; detailed grow start/stop actions still need confirmation. Pro uses a touchscreen, adds automatic light scheduling and shows species-based harvest estimates.

Lighting experiment—not a confirmed Base feature. A cycle of 12 hours on and 12 hours off, triggered by the first button press, is being considered for Virelis Base and possibly Mycelis Base. It isn't included in the current specification or promised for launch.

Virelis Pro estimates timing from species, not environmental sensors. Base does not provide harvest predictions. Accuracy is not yet validated. These are guides, not guaranteed harvest dates.

Does Virelis use environmental sensors?

Virelis is sensorless. Automatic water delivery is intended for both models, but there is no environmental sensing, humidity control or fan control. Neither model has active heating or cooling. Pro estimates are species-based, not a live assessment of the crop.

Offline use and power interruptions

No app or cloud connection is required for growing. Pro can optionally use Wi-Fi for date/time, rather than remote control. No remote monitoring or AI growing features are included at launch.

Power recovery is a design intention, not a tested reliability feature. Base is intended to return in a paused state after power is restored. Pro is intended to recover stored information and obtain date/time through optional Wi-Fi or request manual input. Automatic grow resumption is not promised; this behaviour still needs testing.

Why microgreens?

Little leaves. More ways to use them.

Snip some over eggs, tuck them into a sandwich or add a handful to a salad. Studies have measured vitamins C, E and K1, plus beta-carotene, in microgreens. The amounts vary a lot by variety. [1]

Nutrition research examples — see the refill section for our launch varieties.

Radish.

Peppery leaves for a sandwich or salad. In a study of 25 microgreens, green daikon radish had the highest vitamin E concentration among the samples tested. [1]

Pea shoots.

A sweeter-tasting option to tuck into a wrap. Researchers have also measured vitamin C in pea microgreens grown under controlled conditions. [2]

Red cabbage.

A splash of purple in a bowl of greens. Red cabbage led the vitamin C measurements in that same 25-variety study. Different leaves bring different things to the table. [1]

We haven't lab-tested a Virelis harvest. Seed variety, light, growing conditions, harvest stage, storage and portion size can change what you eat. These findings aren't a promise of health effects or higher nutrition from the machine, and a small garnish doesn't replace a varied diet.

Sources & what they tell us
  1. Xiao and colleagues, 2012 — 25 commercially grown microgreens. Measurements of vitamins and carotenoids, with substantial differences between varieties. This was a food-composition study, not a trial of health outcomes or Virelis.
  2. Balik and colleagues, 2025 — nutritional profiles of six microgreens. Included pea microgreens grown under controlled conditions. The vitamin C result describes those samples, not a guaranteed amount in a home grow.

The growing routine

Start with a mat. Finish with lunch.

The intended setup needs Virelis, pre-seeded mats and water. Mats germinate directly inside the machine without blackout covers. Use up to three mats, one per tier; unused tiers can remain empty.

Each tier has its own pump, fed from a shared reservoir. The tiers operate independently, so different varieties can be grown at different stages. Water does not recirculate. The current prototype still needs manual control of some functions; spray coverage, drip control and the final care routine need more testing.

You'll check the water level visually and top up as needed. Harvest by cutting the greens with scissors: one harvest per mat, then a fresh mat for the next grow. Refilling, draining and cleaning guidance will be confirmed after testing.

Leave room in front to get the trays in and out, and space behind for ventilation. Required clearances still need testing. We'll publish measured external dimensions and weight before launch; the prototype and next-test mat sizes below are not appliance dimensions.

About using Virelis

What you might want to know

Can I make my own Virelis seed mats?

Yes, you may experiment with your own seeds and growing media. Alternatives such as tissue paper are experiments, not validated recommendations. The current prototype uses approximately 112 mm mats; the next planned test is 125 × 125 mm with slightly larger trays.

We'll provide dimensions, basic DIY instructions and troubleshooting support, but material compatibility and successful grows aren't guaranteed. No third-party commercial refills have been validated. The exact documentation is still being decided. This isn't a requirement to buy our mats, use a proprietary digital service or take out a subscription.

Does DIY-friendly mean open source?

DIY-friendly doesn't mean open source. We intend to share refill dimensions, fitting information and basic DIY guidance. This isn't a commitment to release appliance CAD, electronics designs, firmware or manufacturing methods. The exact documents are still being decided.

Do I need a subscription?

No. Our 9-, 18- and 27-mat packs are one-off refills. Any future subscription would be optional; recurring deliveries aren't active.

How many grows does a nine-mat pack cover?

Nine fresh mats provide three full sets when you use all three tiers, with one mat per tier. You can also use fewer tiers or start them on different days. One harvest is intended per mat; this isn't a guarantee of three successful harvests, a particular yield or a harvest date.

What are the mats made of?

Hemp or coconut coir are being considered for the mat, with gum arabic as the intended seed adhesive. Materials and the finished mat design are not final.

We plan to apply seeds by hand and weigh them for each mat, rather than estimate by eye. Seed weights and production checks still need validation. Early production will focus on batch labelling and clean conditions; we don't currently have laboratory testing facilities.

Can I reuse a mat or put it in the organics bin?

Mats are single-use, with one harvest each, not regrowth. We'd like spent mats to go into organics, but that depends on the final materials and local collection rules. Compostability and organic-bin acceptance are not confirmed. We'll publish disposal guidance once those details are checked.

How long do seed mats last in storage?

No storage testing has been completed. We won't publish a shelf life or recommend storage conditions until they've been tested. Pack size isn't a promise of how long mats will keep.

Can I buy one pack at a time?

Yes. We offer 9-, 18- and 27-mat packs as one-off refills. You can try the pack selector in the password-protected sandbox shop; real orders aren't open yet.

Can I choose different microgreen varieties?

Yes. Choose Mixed Greens, Micro Pak Choy, Cress moss or Kohlrabi purple in multiples of three mats per variety. A 9-pack could be 3 + 3 + 3, 6 + 3, or 9 of one variety. The same rule applies to the 18- and 27-mat packs. Groups of three are a packing rule, not a growing requirement; different tiers can grow different varieties.

Mixed Greens is a seed blend on each mat, not separate single-variety mats. Only Mixed Greens has been grown in Virelis so far, with automatic watering incomplete and spray coverage still inadequate. The other choices are being sourced. Exact seed identities, cultivars and blend contents still need confirmation with the distributor.

Will every model have a screen?

Base is screenless, with three independent per-tier light controls under the current manual-lighting plan. Pro uses a touchscreen and adds automatic light scheduling and species-based harvest estimates. The possible Base 12/12 cycle is an experiment, not an included feature. Neither needs an app or cloud connection for growing; Pro's optional Wi-Fi is for date/time. See the model comparison for details.

How will watering and draining work?

Three independent pumps share one reservoir, with one pump per tier and no recirculation. Excess water collects in trays. Emptying those trays at harvest is the current design approach, not validated customer care guidance.

Water levels are currently checked visually. Refill intervals haven't been validated; a dedicated viewing window is being considered, not confirmed. No automatic water-level sensing is promised.

Oversaturation and hygiene from accumulated tray water still need testing, including when tiers start on different days. Final draining and cleaning routines may change; water left for a whole growing cycle has not been established as safe.

How will I clean it?

The proposed between-grows routine uses a small amount of mild soap followed by clean tap water. It hasn't been validated and is not final customer guidance or an instruction to rinse the assembled machine or its electrical parts.

Removable components are intended to be hand-washed, not dishwasher-cleaned, preferably separately from everyday kitchen utensils. The exact washable parts and procedure still need confirmation.

Routine tubing care hasn't been validated. We'd like to avoid routine tubing cleaning, but can't promise maintenance-free tubing. A small cleaning brush is being considered, not confirmed as an included accessory.

Have materials and exposure risks been assessed?

Growing chambers encounter moisture and spores; the water reservoirs are separate. Produce isn't intended to touch the appliance's plastic surfaces directly. Material suitability still needs verification, including possible indirect exposure through water or condensate. That design intention is not a food-safety certification.

No qualified assessment of spores, allergies or mould risks has been completed. Filter effectiveness hasn't been validated; a filter's presence is not proof of effective protection. We aren't making spore-free or allergy-safe claims.

What if a grow fails or looks contaminated?

Don't eat a failed or suspected contaminated grow. These grows should be discarded; contact support for next steps. Product-specific handling and disposal guidance still needs validation.

We'll investigate what happened and assess the appropriate troubleshooting or remedy, including repair, replacement or refund where applicable under consumer law. Varying grow results do not exclude your statutory rights. Consumer Protection guidance.

Are the prototypes ready for customer use or trials?

We plan to have Mycelis Base, Mycelis Pro, Virelis Base and Virelis Pro reviewed by an appropriately qualified electrical/product-safety professional before customer use. No formal approval has been obtained. A professional inspection is not automatically formal product certification.

Customer loans and in-home trials won't begin until electrical safety has been assessed. Market demonstrations are planned to be supervised, but no event or date is confirmed. Registering interest does not book a demonstration, approve a trial or reserve a machine.

How much room will it need?

Leave access in front to remove the trays and space behind for ventilation. Exact clearances haven't been tested, and external dimensions and weight aren't final. Wait for measured specifications before planning a tight spot; mat dimensions are not the machine's footprint.

What power adapter is included, and what will it cost to run?

The planned package includes an external 12 V, 5 A adapter with 60 W rated output capacity. This is the adapter's rating, not measured appliance power draw or wall-plug consumption.

We haven't measured electricity use or running costs. There are no low-energy or cost-per-grow figures to promise yet; we'll publish measured figures after testing.

Will it be noisy or leave moisture on the bench?

On the Virelis prototype, the noticeable sound is from pumps during irrigation rather than a continuous fan. We haven't made acoustic measurements, so we're not promising silent operation or a decibel rating.

The prototype may leave small amounts of moisture on surrounding surfaces. Spray coverage, drip control and moisture handling still need refinement; a dry surrounding bench isn't a validated claim yet.

What room conditions will suit it?

Virelis is intended for ordinary indoor kitchen environments. Neither Base nor Pro actively heats or cools. A suitable operating temperature range hasn't been validated; final guidance will follow testing, not an assumption about how warm a home is.

Does growing indoors make microgreens automatically safe to eat?

No. Seeds, water, growing medium and handling can all introduce contamination. Use seeds sold for edible microgreens and clean drinking water, keep hands and harvesting tools clean, and clean trays between grows. Refrigerate harvested greens. These are basic precautions, not a safety guarantee; we'll publish product-specific care instructions before launch. Microgreen food-safety guidance (PDF).

Can I order one now?

Not yet. We have a physical prototype, but no final appliance price or confirmed release date. The mailing list is where we'll share the next build and announce those details when they're settled.
